# Business model, pricing and scale

## Independent judgment

The unmet need is credible; willingness to pay and repeat use are **unvalidated**. Free regulator access, free company opt-outs and low consumer tolerance for repeated fees create a difficult acquisition model. Frequent unwanted messages do not imply frequent purchases. A subscription risks charging for inactivity or encouraging needless claims. Start with a fixed, finite deliverable; do not offer unlimited case management for KSh199.

Positioning to test: **“Take control of unwanted marketing. Keep the evidence. Choose your next step.”** Explain that PrivacyGuard helps organise information and requests, independently of ODPC. A remedies explanation should include correction, stopping marketing and possible compensation where justified. Do not put an estimated award or “success chance” next to the pay button.

No credible Kenya-specific willingness-to-pay study or acquisition cohort was supplied or independently established. The prices below are experiment hypotheses, not a claim about the market rate.

KSh199 might work for a narrow automated deliverable, a subsidised service or an introductory experiment. It does not support repeated human correspondence by assertion. Keep it off public production pricing until its economics and scope are validated. A company-wide directory search should be reused lawfully rather than bought for every consumer: the observed BRS CR12 fee alone is KSh650. [BRS fee schedule](https://brs.go.ke/fee-schedule-companies-registry/)

Show an indicative price range before users invest effort, then the **exact total and scope before purchase**, after suitability screening. Do not surprise someone with a paywall around their own evidence or the free official link. Fees buy an administrative deliverable, never access to ODPC or an entitlement to a remedy.

## Refund and failure policy proposal

- Unverified recipient or inability to perform the promised work: do not charge, or refund the undelivered service.
- Payment duplicated or captured with no order: reconcile and refund through an authorised finance workflow.
- Company rejects a request or ODPC dismisses a complaint: no automatic success refund if the accurately described service was delivered; explain this before purchase and comply with mandatory rights.
- Consumer cancels before work starts: proposed full refund. After partial work, use a clear lawful policy tied to delivered work, not punitive blanket non-refund language.
- Staff error, wrong recipient or privacy incident: pause service, escalate and provide remedy; refund does not replace incident handling.
- Refund shown as requested → approved → provider processing → confirmed. No “refunded” badge from a button click.

Counsel/accountant must approve consumer terms, cancellation rules, receipts, applicable taxes and tax-inclusive pricing. The numerical model excludes tax; gross revenue must not be confused with usable margin.

## Sensitivity and break-even

Contribution per paid customer, before fixed overhead:

`price × 0.92 − 10 × paid_minutes − 10 − (10 × free_minutes + 6 + 20) / conversion`

At free_minutes1.5 and conversion20%, a499 price leaves aboutKSh144 per payer at10paid minutes;199 loses aboutKSh132. At499 and5% conversion, even10paid minutes loses aboutKSh471. Price changes cannot compensate for unlimited free manual work or poor conversion. See [all sensitivity combinations](data/pricing_sensitivity.csv) and [recalculable generator](data/build_cost_model.py).

For base effort/costs at499, pre-overhead break-even conversion is `(15+6+20)/(499×.92−100−10)` ≈**11.74%**. At199 it is approximately**56.10%**. These are not sales targets endorsed by evidence; fixed costs and taxes raise them further.

## Validation and commercial gates

1. Before development: test comprehension and purchase choices with realistic static boards; ask users what they believe they are buying and whether ODPC is free. Do not prime them with compensation amounts.
2. After legal/privacy clearance: manually time30–50 bounded cases with informed participants; record all unpaid triage and support minutes, not only “happy path” clicks. Use synthetic evidence before that clearance.
3. A later approved pilot should measure paid conversion by acquisition source, contribution after refunds, median/p90 handling time, wrong-recipient incidents, response delivery, repeat marketing at30days, and customer understanding of outcome uncertainty.
4. Proposed expansion gate: positive contribution after realistic labour/CAC for two cohorts; no critical privacy/authority failures;≥90% understanding that payment buys assistance rather than an award; service promises met for≥95% of accepted pilot orders. These are recommended decision thresholds, not industry benchmarks.

Avoid selling complaint data, advertiser access to complainants, or outcomes-based lead commissions. A future sponsor-funded access model may lower consumer cost but needs a conflict policy, no sponsor case access, and separate validation. Defer subscriptions, automated mass claims and award collection/custody.

The KSh999 rows in the scale CSV retain the same10-minute assumption solely for price sensitivity; they are not a pack-service cost forecast. For a pack with45paid minutes, the base formula yields aboutKSh254 contribution per payer before fixed costs; at75minutes it loses aboutKSh46. Measure preparation, corrections and support together.
